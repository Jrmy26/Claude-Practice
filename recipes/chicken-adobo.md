# Chicken Adobo

Trimmed boneless thighs braised in soy, vinegar and plenty of garlic, then seared and glazed in the reduced sauce, give classic adobo flavour and a measured portion of rice at 54 g protein and under 500 kcal.

**Serves:** 4 | **Prep:** 15 min | **Cook:** 45 min
**Diet:** dairy-free, egg-free
**Contains:** soy, wheat (both soy sauces)

## Ingredients
### Rice
- 2/3 cup (120 g) jasmine rice, uncooked
- 3/4 cup plus 1 Tbsp (200 mL) water

### Main
- 2 lb 3 oz (1 kg) boneless skinless chicken thighs, weighed after trimming

### Braise
- 1/3 cup (80 mL) light soy sauce
- 1 Tbsp (15 mL) dark soy sauce
- 12 garlic cloves (45 g), peeled and lightly smashed
- 1 cup (240 mL) water
- 4 dried bay leaves (0.6 g)
- 1 tsp (3 g) whole black peppercorns
- 2 tsp (9 g) packed brown sugar
- 1/2 cup (120 mL) white vinegar

### Cooking
- 2 tsp (10 mL) canola or other neutral oil, divided

## Equipment
- Small saucepan with a tight-fitting lid
- Wide heavy pot or deep 12-inch (30 cm) skillet with a lid, about 4 to 5 qt (4 to 5 L), wide enough to hold the chicken in one layer
- 12-inch (30 cm) nonstick skillet
- Instant-read thermometer
- 4 meal prep containers

## Steps
1. Rinse the rice in a sieve under cold running water, swirling it with your fingers, until the water runs almost clear.
2. Put the rice and its water in the small saucepan and bring to a boil over high heat, uncovered, for 3 to 4 minutes, until bubbles break across the whole surface.
3. Cover the pan, turn the heat to low and cook for 12 minutes without lifting the lid, until no water remains when you tilt the pan at the end.
4. While the rice cooks, cut away the skin and any thick white fat pads from the chicken, then cut each thigh in half crosswise so the pieces cook evenly.
5. Lay the chicken in the pot in a snug single layer, then add the light and dark soy sauces, garlic, water, bay leaves, peppercorns and brown sugar.
6. Pour the vinegar over the top and do not stir.
7. When the rice is done, turn off its heat and leave it covered for at least 10 minutes, until the grains are tender and separate; it can wait, covered, until you pack the containers.
8. Bring the pot to a boil over medium-high heat, uncovered and without stirring, for 5 to 7 minutes, until it bubbles across the whole surface.
9. Keep it boiling uncovered, still without stirring, for 3 minutes, until the sharp vinegar smell softens; boiling the vinegar before stirring cooks off its harsh raw edge.
10. Stir, cover and simmer over low heat for 20 to 25 minutes, turning the pieces halfway, until a knife slides easily into the thickest piece and it reads at least 165°F (74°C); thighs are most tender at 175 to 185°F (80 to 85°C).
11. Transfer the chicken to a plate with tongs, leaving the garlic, bay leaves and peppercorns in the pot.
12. Tilt the pot and spoon off the clear fat pooled on the surface of the sauce, until only small droplets remain.
13. Boil the sauce uncovered over medium-high heat for 8 to 12 minutes, stirring now and then, until it has reduced by about half, looks glossy and lightly coats the back of a spoon.
14. Meanwhile, pat the chicken dry with paper towels so it browns instead of steaming.
15. Divide the chicken into 2 batches, and the oil into the same number of equal shares.
16. Heat one share of the oil in the nonstick skillet over medium-high heat for 1 minute, until it shimmers.
17. Add one batch of chicken, smooth side down, and sear without moving it for 2 to 3 minutes, until the edges turn deep brown; lower the heat if it smokes, because the soy-soaked surface scorches quickly.
18. Turn the pieces and sear for 1 to 2 minutes, until the second side is browned, then return them to the plate. Repeat the last two steps and this one for any remaining batches.
19. If the sauce does not yet coat the back of a spoon, keep boiling it until it does, then lower the heat to medium-low.
20. Return the chicken and any plate juices to the sauce and turn the pieces for 1 minute, until each one is glazed and the sauce clings to it.
21. Fluff the rice with a fork and divide it among 4 containers.
22. Divide the chicken among the containers and spoon the sauce, garlic and peppercorns over it, leaving the bay leaves in the pot.

## Substitutions
- Chicken thighs, if your pack is fattier: these macros assume thighs trimmed to about 4 g fat per 100 g raw. If the label shows 6 g or more, use 1 lb 2 oz (500 g) thighs and 1 lb 2 oz (500 g) boneless skinless chicken breast, which keeps a serving at about 495 kcal with 58 g protein. Cut the breast into chunks the size of the thigh halves and add them after the thighs have simmered for 10 minutes so they stay juicy, still cooking them to 165°F (74°C).
- All chicken breast: 2 lb 3 oz (1 kg) boneless skinless chicken breast gives about 480 kcal and 61 g protein per serving, but breast dries out faster and turns stringy over several days of reheating. Simmer it for only 12 to 15 minutes, until 165°F (74°C).
- Skin-on boneless thighs (often sold as boneless chicken leg): buy about 2 lb 14 oz (1.3 kg), remove the skin and fat pads, and weigh out 1 kg after trimming. Leaving the skin on adds roughly 175 kcal per serving, taking it to about 650 kcal, well over 500.
- White vinegar: Filipino cane vinegar (sukang Iloko), the same amount, if you find it; it is slightly milder and rounder, with no change in calories.
- Dark soy sauce: leave it out and use 1 Tbsp (15 mL) more light soy sauce; the sauce is paler, less glossy and slightly saltier.
- Less salt: use 1/4 cup (60 mL) light soy sauce and add 4 tsp (20 mL) more water; most of the colour comes from the dark soy, so the look barely changes.
- Brown sugar: the same amount of white sugar, or leave it out for a sharper, more sour adobo that is about 8 kcal lighter per serving.
- Whole peppercorns: 1/2 tsp (1 g) coarsely ground black pepper, which spreads the heat through the sauce instead of in occasional bites.
- Jasmine rice: the same weight of uncooked brown rice, at about the same calories with more fibre; cook it with 1 1/3 cups (320 mL) water for 35 to 40 minutes before resting.
- Lighter base: replace the rice with 1 lb (450 g) cauliflower, grated into rice-sized pieces and steamed or microwaved until just tender; a serving drops to about 400 kcal with 54 g protein.
- Larger rice portion: 200 g (about 1 cup) uncooked jasmine rice for the batch brings a serving to about 550 kcal, over 500. The 120 g in the recipe leaves a small margin under 500 kcal; 140 g is the most that fits, at about 498 kcal.

## Storage
Leave the containers uncovered until they stop steaming, then cover and refrigerate within 1 hour of cooking, since cooked rice left warm can grow bacteria. Keep for up to 4 days; the adobo tastes even better on day two as the sauce soaks in. Freeze for up to 2 months. The sauce sets to a jelly in the fridge from the chicken's gelatin and melts back when heated. Reheat loosely covered in the microwave with 1 Tbsp (15 mL) water sprinkled over the rice, for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring halfway, until steaming and 165°F (74°C) in the centre.
